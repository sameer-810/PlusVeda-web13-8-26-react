## What gets rejected, and why

Fourteen more were downloaded and thrown away on inspection. The recurring
failures are worth knowing before anyone searches for a replacement:

- **Wrong country.** A Vietnamese chemist with Vietnamese signage in shot; a US
  drugstore under a neon "PRESCRIPTIONS" sign. Both read instantly as not-India
  to the person we are selling to.
- **Wrong century.** Stock libraries are full of vintage apothecaries — dark
  wood, glass bottles with Latin labels, mortar and pestle. Searching
  "pharmacy" returns more of these than real ones.
- **Wrong dispensing format.** American amber pill bottles. Indian chemists
  dispense strips, and the wrong format on the page is a small credibility leak.
- **Not a pharmacy at all.** A warehouse worker with chemical drums came back
  under "pharmacy inventory".

## Where photographs are used, and where they are not

Photographs set the scene: the eight-card feature grid, where a picture only
has to say what kind of work this is.

The product itself is shown as real screenshots — hero, "what is Plusveda" and
the tabbed showcase — at a size where a chemist can read the numbers. That
split is deliberate. A 1440px application screen reduced to a 250px card is
grey noise; the first build of the grid used screenshots and had to be redone.
